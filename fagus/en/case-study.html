Case study · demonstration check

How accessible are three public pages on anaf.ro

A short check of a three-page sample from the Romanian tax agency's website, carried out on 1 October 2026. It shows the kind of issues an accessibility audit brings up, and how they get fixed.

This is a demonstration check, not a full audit.

I looked at only three pages, once, in a single browser. Automated tools catch only part of the accessibility issues on a page; the rest only show up in manual testing and testing with assistive technology. The results describe the pages as they were on the day of the check and are not an assessment of the whole site's conformance.

What was checked

Date of the check: 1 October 2026. Each page was loaded a handful of times; the site was not crawled.

  1. Home page: https://www.anaf.ro/
  2. Company lookup by tax ID (the register of VAT-registered businesses): https://www.anaf.ro/RegistruTVA/
  3. Downloadable forms page („Toate formularele, cu explicații”, reached from the taxpayer assistance menu): https://static.anaf.ro/static/10/Anaf/AsistentaContribuabili_r/Toateformularele/Toate_Formularele.html

Tools and method

  • axe-core 4.13.0: WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 rules at levels A and AA, plus best practices.
  • Lighthouse 13.5.0: Accessibility category, desktop profile.
  • Google Chrome 154 on Linux, in a 1366 × 900 px window.
  • Keyboard-only navigation: the first 45 presses of the Tab key on each page, noting which element receives focus and whether you can see it.
  • 200% zoom (and a 320 px width, the size of a small phone): whether the page scrolls sideways and whether elements overlap.
  • Contrast and form labels: the tools' results, backed up by inspecting the fields on the page.

Not checked: screen readers, the search results (the form was not submitted), the content of the PDF documents, other browsers and real phones.

In total, axe-core reported 51 elements that fail WCAG criteria and another 18 that depart from best practice. The keyboard and zoom checks added issues that automated tools cannot see; those are covered under points 3, 4 and 5 below.

The five most important issues

  1. 01

    Images and buttons with no text description

    What's wrong
    21 images and 6 image buttons have no alternative text. They include the display controls on the home page (brightness, for example) and the verification-code image on the tax ID lookup. There is a “Listen” button next to the code; I did not test whether the audio version works.
    Who it affects
    People who are blind or have low vision and use a screen reader. The software only announces “image” or “button”, without saying what it is for.
    How to fix it
    Every image that carries meaning gets a short description (the alt attribute), and every button gets a clear name. Purely decorative images are marked alt="" so they are skipped. The verification code needs an alternative that does not rely on sight.

  2. 02

    Links that don't say where they go

    What's wrong
    15 links have no name at all: they contain only an image with no description, or they are empty. Among them are the logo that leads back to the home page (on pages 2 and 3) and the tax calendar banner.
    Who it affects
    Screen reader users hear just “link”, or a long web address. People using voice control have no name to say.
    How to fix it
    The image inside the link gets alternative text naming the destination, such as “ANAF – home page”. Empty links are removed.

  3. 03

    The page language is missing or wrong

    What's wrong
    The home page and the tax ID lookup do not declare a language. The forms page is declared as English (lang="en") although the text is Romanian; the automated tools did not flag this, it came up on inspection.
    Who it affects
    A screen reader picks its pronunciation from the declared language. Romanian text read with an English voice is hard to follow. The browser's automatic translation can get it wrong too.
    How to fix it
    A single change in the page template: <html lang="ro">.

  4. 04

    Keyboard users lose track of where they are

    What's wrong
    The home page has no “Skip to content” link, and 35 of the first 45 presses of Tab land on submenu links hidden off screen, so you cannot see where you are. On the forms page, the focus outline has been switched off for the search field and the category buttons (10 of the 12 elements). On the tax ID lookup the focus is visible and the form takes 7 steps to get through.
    Who it affects
    People who cannot use a mouse: those with motor disabilities or a tremor, and anyone who works from the keyboard alone.
    How to fix it
    Add a “Skip to content” link at the top of the page. Keep hidden submenus out of the tab order until they are opened. Never remove the focus outline (outline: none) without putting something visible in its place.

  5. 05

    At 200% zoom the page no longer fits the window

    What's wrong
    The home page and the tax ID lookup have a fixed width: at 200% the page scrolls sideways, and on the home page the fixed buttons along the edges cover part of the content. The forms page adapts properly, down to the width of a small phone.
    Who it affects
    People with low vision who enlarge the page, and anyone visiting on a phone.
    How to fix it
    A flexible layout instead of fixed widths: add a meta viewport tag, use relative widths, and rearrange the fixed elements on narrow screens.

Other observations

  • Form labels. On the tax ID lookup, both fields have properly linked labels. On the forms page, the search field only has hint text inside it, which disappears once you start typing.
  • Contrast. The tools did not confirm any contrast problem. 19 elements could not be assessed automatically (text over images, for instance) and I did not measure them by hand.
  • Structure. None of the three pages has a main heading (h1). On the home page 5 lists are built incorrectly, and on the tax ID lookup one frame (iframe) has no title.
  • What is already there. The site has an accessibility statement, a panel of display options and a read-aloud feature.

What this means for your website

The same five kinds of issues turn up on a great many websites, small business sites included. Most of them are fixed once, in the page template, and stay fixed.
